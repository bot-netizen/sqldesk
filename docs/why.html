Why SQLDesk

An honest account of where this sits, what it does better than the alternatives, and what the alternatives still do better. If you only need one of the things below, one of those other tools is probably the right answer.

Six reasons, each with its receipt

01

Your viewers are free, and always will be

Self-hosted with no seat count, no viewer tier and no license server to call home to. The tools that charge for viewers get expensive precisely when your work succeeds and more people start looking at it.

One published comparison puts a 10-editor, 10-viewer
setup on a notebook-BI platform at $27,000 a year.
SQLDesk: the cost of the box you run it on.

02

Actively built, not merely kept alive

SQLDesk forked Redash, which has been in maintenance mode for years — dependency bumps and security patches rather than new work. That makes the fork additive rather than divergent: there is no fast-moving upstream to fight, and the features people have been asking for are simply unbuilt.

Redash upstream: roughly one release a year since 2022,
community forum read-only, ~7 volunteer maintainers.

03

It doesn't phone home

Not a policy — a removal. The upstream shipped hard-coded endpoints that POSTed usage statistics, and separately your admin's name and email, to servers you don't control. Both are gone. Version checking exists but is off until you point it at an endpoint of your own.

Removed: version.redash.io/api/report (usage data)
Removed: version.redash.io/subscribe (admin name + email)

04

Performance claims come with numbers

Everything in the query path gets measured before and after, on real data volumes, and the figures are published. No "blazing fast" — just what it was, what it is, and how it was measured.

Cached 27MB result: 547ms → 99ms
Cache lookup on a year of history: 66.7ms → 0.064ms
See the full workings →

05

Apache 2.0, with the provenance kept straight

A permissive licence with an explicit patent grant, which BSD 2-Clause does not provide. The inherited code keeps its original copyright and licence text, reproduced in full — because the alternative is a licence violation dressed up as a rebrand.

LICENSE → Apache 2.0  ·  LICENSE.redash → BSD 2-Clause, verbatim
NOTICE → attribution, and an explicit "not affiliated" statement

06

One tool, not four stitched together

SQL, dashboards and alerts today; notebooks and Kafka streams next — under one permission model, one login and one deployment. The value isn't any single surface, it's that a note can read a saved query, join it against a stream, and publish the result to a dashboard without leaving the product or re-declaring who may see what.

Worth saying out loud

Databricks acquired Redash in 2020. SQLDesk is building, from that lineage, toward a subset of what Databricks does — with one docker compose up, no lakehouse commitment and no per-seat bill.

What other tools do better

Breadth is the bet, and breadth has a cost. If one of these is your whole problem, use the specialist.

Briefer, for notebooks today

Open-source notebooks and dashboards with real-time multiplayer editing, shipping now. SQLDesk's Notes are still ahead of us on the roadmap.

Timeplus, for true streaming

A purpose-built streaming engine with push-based dashboards over SSE. SQLDesk starts with bounded reads from Kafka, which is a smaller claim honestly made.

Metabase, for non-technical users

The best point-and-click question builder in open source. SQLDesk assumes somebody on the team writes SQL.

Grafana, for time-series and ops

If your dashboards are about infrastructure and alerting on metrics, that is what Grafana was built for and SQLDesk was not.
